Description:
The Bangkok Chaozhou Cemetery is located in Sathorn, at a walking distance from the Muslim graveyard of the Java-Mosque and the other Chinese cemeteries in Bang Rak. It is by far the largest Chinese cemetery in Bangkok and among the largest Chinese cemeteries in Thailand. Tombs and tombstone inscriptions are of a relatively uniform style. Most tombs are single-person-tombs, although many tombstones mention the names of two or more people. Actually, the same tombstone inscription, referring to two or more people, can be found several times in the cemetery. This, interestingly, allows to link tombstones in a simple way into a family tree. Unfortunately, from the tombstone inscription it is not obvious, who of the deceased mentioned on the tombstone is burried in this particular tomb. In a few cases, the deceased mentioned on the tombstone are not even burried in this cemetery. They can be burried in other parts of Thailand or in China. The tombstone thus allows to venerate ancestors in a way that is similar to the veneration of ancestors through ancestral tablets.

Hypotheses:
We hypothesize, that this particular usage of tombstones on the Chaozhou cemetery in Bangkok, i.e. the reference to people not burried in this tomb and not in this cemetery, reflects the fact that many or most Chaozhou families don't have, in their home or in their clan shrine, ancestral tablets which would allow them to venerate those ancestors who have not been burried in this cemetery. Actually we know very little about ancestral tablets of the Chaozhou community of Bangkok. Although there are a few Chinese temples in Bangkok that are home to hundreds of ancestral tablets, the total number of ancestral tablets doesn't seem to match the total number of tombstones.
